# Tekteon Fissureflesh: Understanding Arena’s Poison Cloud Wall

**Description:** Explore Tekteon’s Arena strengths, Poison Cloud synergy, build priorities, and ways to counter Mercurial after RAID’s Update 11.75.

You keep attacking, but the enemy health bar barely moves. That is the kind of Arena matchup that makes Tekteon Fissureflesh frustrating to face. For a team relying on a single burst of damage, his Poison Cloud can disrupt the plan before it gets started.

His strength comes from the way his abilities work together. Tekteon can protect teammates, draw single-target attacks, and put pressure on enemy skill cooldowns. Beating him starts with understanding which defensive layer you need to address before committing your finishing attack.

## Poison Cloud and the cost of attacking at the wrong moment

While its protection is active, Poison Cloud forces ordinary incoming attacks to become weak hits. Teams that depend on critical hits can lose much of their pressure exactly when they need to finish a target.

Tekteon’s Sulfurous Breath passive adds another complication: enemy hits increase his MAX HP, with weak hits providing a larger increase. The referenced skill description lists gains of 5% and 10% respectively, stacking up to 100%. The passive also reduces damage received from critical hits.

**The practical lesson:** do not spend every multi-hit ability simply to see whether he goes down. Without a follow-up plan to remove protection or apply control, you can exhaust your offense while leaving the opponent in command of the fight.

More MAX HP does not automatically mean a full heal or immortality. Distinguish increased durability from recovery when assessing the matchup.

## From team protection to skill lockout

Flaming Bulwark establishes Tekteon’s position with Taunt, Poison Cloud, and a Shield for his allies, followed by an Extra Turn. Taunt restricts target selection for single-target skills; it does not redirect every AoE attack exclusively onto Tekteon.

The Extra Turn creates an opportunity to use Volcanic Presence immediately. This two-hit AoE attack can increase enemy skill cooldowns. If those effects land, the opposing team may lose access to key abilities just when it needs a revive, cleanse, or counterattack.

An opening sequence worth considering is **Flaming Bulwark → Volcanic Presence**. Read the battle before committing, however: enemies that are already controlled, or about to receive support from a cleanser, may call for a different skill order.

His A1, Fissureblades, maintains pressure through AoE attacks and a chance to Stun. Tekteon therefore retains a useful action while his stronger skills recharge. [See the skill overview on HellHades](https://hellhades.com/raid/champions/tekteon-fissureflesh/).

## Mercurial after 11.75: a defensive layer with a pressure point

Mercurial adds Total Guard, making it harder to open with an important attack or effect. Combined with Poison Cloud, it asks opponents to think about both the order in which they dismantle protection and the timing of their control skills.

Older guides need one important update: **version 11.75 allows Provoke to bypass Total Guard. A Champion under Provoke also cannot apply or refresh Total Guard stacks at the beginning of their turn.** Plarium states that the other Total Guard effects and the wider Mercurial set remain unchanged. [Read the official patch notes](https://forum.plarium.com/raid-shadow-legends/843_news/43791_new-update-version-11-75-0/).

That change makes Provoke an option worth exploring against a Mercurial-equipped Tekteon. This is a tactical assessment, not a guaranteed win: bypassing Total Guard does not automatically bypass Resistance, Block Debuffs, or an ability’s conditions. If Provoke depends on an attack, Poison Cloud remains an interaction to check.

## Build priorities: choose the role before completing the set

For a team that needs to control the pace of battle, Tanne Hub recommends starting with three priorities: **HP to survive, Speed to take turns, and Accuracy to land effects**. Resistance is an additional investment when your gear allows it and you know which opponents you need to resist.

For a damage-focused build, invest in Critical Rate and Critical Damage as well. Watch the tradeoff: extra damage may leave Tekteon too slow or unable to control the targets you regularly encounter.

Avoid treating any fixed HP, Speed, or Accuracy target as a universal recipe. Reviewing losses gives you more useful information:

- Dying before the first turn: reassess durability and support from teammates.
- Taking turns but getting resisted: review Accuracy and target selection.
- Surviving without closing out fights: evaluate the entire team’s damage.
- Repeatedly losing buffs or getting locked out: review turn order and support options.

A complete Mercurial set with unsuitable stats may not solve these problems. Compare its actual results with your existing gear.

## How to approach the matchup

**First, find a way to neutralize Poison Cloud.** HP Burn is a relevant interaction, and buff removal offers another route. Simply bringing an HP Burn Champion is not enough, though: you still need to land the effect through protection and Resistance.

**Next, control the response.** Once you create an opening, have a follow-up ready so Tekteon cannot immediately rebuild his position. Choose Provoke, cooldown increases, or other control tools around your actual lineup and each ability’s conditions.

**Then commit to the finish.** Multi-hit attacks can help deal with Total Guard, but keep damage and follow-up effects ready. Breaking the first layer and then surrendering the initiative can force you to start again.

In Live Arena, consider Tekteon’s potential teammates throughout the draft. A lineup with cleansing, revival, or additional protection presents a very different challenge from Tekteon on his own. If your remaining picks cannot produce a consistent answer, spending your ban on him is a reasonable decision.

## Tanne Hub’s take

Tekteon is frustrating because he can make opponents spend several turns and skills just to create an attacking opportunity. His value depends on gear quality, teammates, and how effectively each player uses that opening.

This article does not establish verified win-rate or pick-and-ban data, so calling him the Champion who “dominates the entire meta” would overstate the evidence. The practical conclusion is that **facing Tekteon requires a clear plan for defensive layers and turn control**. Following Update 11.75, Provoke deserves consideration in that plan against Mercurial builds.
